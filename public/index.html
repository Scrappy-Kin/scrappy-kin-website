Why Scrappy Kin exists

Data brokers are companies that collect, package, and sell personal information about people — often without their knowledge or consent.

Most people technically have the right to opt out of these databases. But the process is scattered across hundreds of sites and forms, which means most people never do it.

Scrappy Kin exists to change that.

Instead of asking you to upload the exact personal data you’re trying to protect, Scrappy Kin helps you exercise your privacy rights yourself while keeping your personal information out of our hands.

How it works

1

We show you where to act

Data broker opt-outs are spread across hundreds of companies.

Scrappy Kin helps surface the right links, forms, and next steps so you don’t have to hunt them down yourself.

2

The app helps with the tedious parts

It can generate requests, track what you’ve done, and keep everything organized.

The goal is to make privacy something you can actually keep up with, even on a busy day.

3

You send the requests

You stay in control of the final action.

Requests go directly from you to the broker, and responses come back to you.

4

We never ask you to hand us your personal data

Scrappy Kin is built around a simple idea: exercising your privacy rights should not require trusting another company with your personal data.

We don’t ask you to hand over your identity documents, addresses, or other personal details for us to store and manage.

5

Your effort can help others too

People can optionally contribute non-personal information about how brokers behave — things like whether a form worked, how long it took, or where friction appears.

No personal data is included.

Over time this creates a shared, open-source knowledge base about broker behavior, helping surface bad actors and making it easier for journalists, researchers, and regulators to see where the system is failing.

Contributing to a more private world for everyone.

About Scrappy Kin

Scrappy Kin is built by privacy advocates who believe privacy is a fundamental human right, and that exercising that right should not require money, expertise, or blind trust in another company.

The goal isn’t a magic privacy service.

The goal is a simple tool that helps anyone exercise their privacy rights while shining light on how the data broker ecosystem actually behaves.
